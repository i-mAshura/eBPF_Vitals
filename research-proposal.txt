# eBPF-VITAL: Verifier-in-the-Loop Adaptation for Neurosymbolic Intrusion Detection

**A Research Proposal**

---

## Abstract

Kernel-resident machine learning for intrusion detection is currently built by *working around* the eBPF verifier: models are shrunk, quantized, and unrolled by hand until they happen to pass static acceptance, and online updates are pushed to BPF maps in the hope that shape and range invariants still hold. This proposal inverts that relationship. We treat the eBPF verifier's abstract-interpretation acceptance boundary as a first-class, differentiable constraint inside the learning process itself, rather than a post-hoc filter applied after training. eBPF-VITAL (Verifier-in-the-Loop Adaptation for Neurosymbolic Intrusion Detection) contributes: (1) a learned, calibrated surrogate of the eBPF verifier's abstract interpreter, differentiable with respect to a candidate program's structure and parameters; (2) verifier-constrained architecture search and distillation, which discovers the most expressive kernel-loadable neurosymbolic detector rather than the smallest model that happens to fit; (3) online continual adaptation expressed as constrained optimization — every weight update is projected onto the verifier-acceptance manifold *before* it reaches a live BPF map, giving a formal loadability guarantee that hot-patching approaches lack; and (4) a tight microsecond-scale coupling between an in-kernel temporal-logic monitor's partial evaluation state and the neural gate's attention, closing the neurosymbolic loop at kernel timescale instead of through a userspace round trip. Supporting infrastructure — verdict-aware LSM provenance capture, graduated enforcement, a self-protecting meta-monitor, and kernel-version-portable bundles — is retained from prior scoping but is explicitly secondary to the verifier-in-the-loop mechanism, which is the paper's central, defensible novelty claim.

---

## 1. Problem Statement and Motivation

Advanced persistent threat (APT) detection increasingly relies on whole-system provenance graphs and learned models over them (HOLMES, Kairos, FLASH, ProvDetector). Two independent trends push this detection logic toward the kernel: (a) userspace provenance pipelines are too slow and too displaced from ground truth to catch fast, living-off-the-land attacker behavior before damage occurs, and (b) eBPF has matured into a programmable, verified extension mechanism that can run classifiers and monitors directly at the kernel's decision points (LSM hooks).

However, every attempt to move ML into the kernel has been *fit-first*: train in float, quantize, prune, unroll loops, hand-shrink until the eBPF verifier's static analysis (stack depth, instruction/complexity budget, bounded-loop proofs, pointer and value-range tracking) accepts the program. This produces two structural weaknesses: the model that survives is whatever shrinking happened to leave, not the best model under the constraint; and any later adaptation (drift response, retraining, online fine-tuning) risks silently invalidating verifier-checked invariants unless the update is re-verified from scratch, which is exactly what hot map-patching schemes try to avoid.

**The core research question:** can the eBPF verifier's acceptance region be made a *training signal* — differentiable, queryable, and projectable — so that model search, distillation, and online adaptation are all conducted *inside* the constraint rather than filtered by it afterward?

## 2. Core Mechanism

### 2.1 A differentiable surrogate of the verifier

The Linux eBPF verifier performs bounded static analysis via abstract interpretation over several abstract domains (signed/unsigned interval domains at multiple bit widths, a tristate bit-tracking domain, pointer/type state, loop-bound proofs). This process is well studied formally (PREVAIL, Agni, tristate numbers) but is not differentiable and not cheap to query repeatedly during training.

We build $\hat{V}_\theta$: a learned surrogate, structured as a graph neural network over a candidate program's control-flow/data-flow graph, trained to predict (a) verifier acceptance probability, (b) the residual instruction/complexity budget if accepted, and (c) which specific abstract-domain check is predicted to fail if rejected (for gradient shaping). $\hat{V}_\theta$ is periodically recalibrated against the real verifier (via `bpftool`/libbpf load-and-check cycles) on a held-out and adversarially mined set of near-boundary programs, since the true acceptance region is not smooth and the surrogate's job is to approximate a decision boundary it cannot see directly.

### 2.2 Verifier-constrained architecture search and distillation

Instead of shrinking a trained model post hoc, model discovery is posed as a constrained/penalized optimization:

$$\min_{\phi} \; \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}(\phi) + \lambda \cdot \text{ReLU}\big(1 - \hat{V}_\theta(\phi)\big) + \mu \cdot \text{ReLU}\big(C(\phi) - B\big)$$

where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}$ is the detection loss (carried over from the userspace neurosymbolic core, including the STL-robustness term), $\hat{V}_\theta(\phi)$ is the surrogate's acceptance probability for the architecture/weight encoding $\phi$, $C(\phi)$ is predicted verifier complexity cost, and $B$ is the kernel's per-program budget. This is differentiable NAS/distillation under a hardware constraint (in the tradition of DARTS, FBNet, ProxylessNAS, MicroNAS) but with the "hardware" being the verifier's abstract-interpretation acceptance region rather than a latency/memory table.

### 2.3 Constrained continual adaptation

Online drift response is reframed as projected gradient/mirror descent: a candidate update $\Delta\phi$ is computed from new labeled or weakly-labeled provenance slices, then projected onto (an estimate of) the verifier-acceptance manifold before being written to the live parameter map:

$$\phi_{t+1} = \Pi_{\hat{V}_\theta \geq \tau} \big( \phi_t - \eta \nabla \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}(\phi_t) \big)$$

Every $k$ updates, the projected point is checked against the *real* verifier before being committed to the running program (canary-style), giving a two-tier guarantee: fast, surrogate-checked updates between real-verifier checkpoints, with periodic ground-truth confirmation and rollback if the surrogate has drifted.

### 2.4 Kernel-timescale neurosymbolic coupling

The bounded pt-LTL/MTL monitor plane exposes partial evaluation state (which subformulas are pending, satisfied, or falsified) directly to the neural gate's input/attention *within the kernel*, via shared BPF map state read on the same tail-call chain — no userspace round trip. This is what makes the system neurosymbolic in a load-bearing sense: the two layers are in a tight control loop at microsecond granularity, not a two-stage pipeline joined by an escalation event.

## 3. Supporting Architecture (retained infrastructure)

These planes are necessary system context but are *not* the paper's novelty claim — they should be described concisely and evaluated primarily for overhead/correctness, not framed as contributions in themselves.

- **Verdict-aware LSM provenance capture.** BPF LSM hooks for combined argument+verdict capture; kernel-resolved objects avoid the TOCTOU race that syscall-entry/kprobe hooks are subject to; live provenance graph and taint tags in BPF maps, scoped per cgroup.
- **Graduated enforcement with shadow mode.** allow → rate-limit → cgroup quarantine → kill, with a logged-but-not-enforced shadow mode for safe rollout.
- **Self-protection meta-monitor.** LSM hooks on `bpf()`, program load, and map access; a watchdog heartbeat map for probe-detachment detection. This must be scoped honestly (Section 6): it protects against an attacker with *weaker* privilege than the monitor's own load-time privilege, not against an attacker who already has equal or greater kernel access.
- **Kernel-version-portable bundles.** Quantized weights, formula weights, and per-kernel-version compiled variants, since verifier acceptance is not stable across kernel versions even for semantically identical programs.

## 4. Things To Do (research and engineering tasks)

1. Instrument the real eBPF verifier (via a patched `bpftool`/libbpf harness) to log, for a large corpus of generated and mutated candidate programs, the full trace of abstract-domain decisions, not just accept/reject — this trace is the surrogate's training signal.
2. Build the candidate-program representation (graph encoding of BPF bytecode/CFG suitable for GNN input) and the surrogate architecture $\hat{V}_\theta$.
3. Implement the verifier-constrained NAS/distillation loop against the userspace neurosymbolic core as teacher.
4. Implement the constrained online-update projection and the periodic real-verifier checkpoint/rollback mechanism.
5. Implement the in-kernel pt-LTL/MTL subformula-DAG monitor with tail-call chaining and map-resident state.
6. Wire partial-evaluation monitor state into the neural gate's kernel-resident input path.
7. Build the verdict-aware LSM provenance/taint capture plane and the graduated enforcement plane (shadow mode first).
8. Build the self-protection meta-monitor and heartbeat mechanism, with an explicit, written-down threat model of what privilege asymmetry it assumes.
9. Build the kernel-version-portable bundle/build system (CO-RE/BTF-based) and a CI matrix across target LTS kernels.
10. Design the adversarial mining procedure that finds near-boundary programs to keep the surrogate calibrated (this is likely to be its own contribution/sub-problem).
11. Design the CEGIS-style red-team loop for evasion evaluation, with an explicit, bounded adversary capability model and a fully isolated (snapshot-based) execution sandbox — no synthesized program should reach a shared or production kernel without a human-reviewed diff plus a passing bounded-spec check.

## 5. Things To Prove (formal claims)

1. **Projection soundness:** if $\phi_t$ is verifier-loadable and the projection step is exact (or the surrogate's error is bounded by a known $\epsilon$), then $\phi_{t+1}$ is verifier-loadable (or loadable with probability $\geq 1-\delta$ under a stated surrogate-error model). This is the paper's central formal claim and needs a precise statement of what "projection" means when the true manifold is only accessible through a learned approximation.
2. **Budget-scheduling soundness/completeness tradeoff (carried over from the monitor-priority plane):** a precise statement of what property survives when monitors are scheduled under a cycle budget — soundness for the scheduled fragment, with completeness explicitly *not* claimed for deprioritized properties, plus a bound on how much detection coverage can be adversarially suppressed by inducing budget contention.
3. **Surrogate calibration bound:** a statement (empirical or PAC-style) of how surrogate acceptance-probability error relates to real-verifier false-accept rate, and how that error compounds over $k$ projected update steps before the next real-verifier checkpoint.
4. **Provenance/taint soundness under cgroup scoping:** a statement of what taint-propagation guarantees hold and fail to hold across cgroup/namespace boundaries (lateral movement is the obvious failure mode to characterize precisely, not just mention).
5. **Self-protection privilege ordering:** an explicit statement of the minimum privilege asymmetry required for the meta-monitor's guarantees to hold, and a proof (or a clearly labeled non-proof, i.e., an assumption) that the watchdog cannot be trivially disabled by the same actor capable of disabling the monitors it watches.

## 6. Things To Verify (empirical/feasibility checks — do these *before* committing to full-scale implementation)

1. How faithful can a GNN-based verifier surrogate actually be against the real verifier's abstract interpretation, measured as surrogate-vs-real agreement rate on held-out and adversarially mined programs? This is the single result that determines whether Section 2 is viable at all.
2. What model capacity (hidden width, layer count, quantization level) is actually achievable inside a single tail-called eBPF program's instruction/complexity budget, empirically, not estimated — build the smallest real integer MLP that passes the real verifier and measure its budget consumption precisely.
3. Whether `bpf_timer`-based windowing meets the pt-LTL/MTL monitor's timing-window needs under load (timer re-arm delay under CPU contention).
4. Whether kernel-resident coupling between monitor partial-evaluation state and the neural gate actually reduces end-to-end detection latency versus a userspace-mediated version, and by how much.
5. Whether verifier acceptance behavior differs enough across target LTS kernels to require per-kernel-version retraining of the surrogate, or whether one surrogate transfers with recalibration only.
6. Whether the self-protection watchdog produces an acceptable false-positive rate under normal eBPF program churn (legitimate tools attaching/detaching probes).

## 7. Things To Create (concrete artifacts)

- The verifier-instrumentation harness and the resulting labeled corpus of candidate programs with full abstract-domain decision traces.
- The differentiable verifier surrogate $\hat{V}_\theta$ and its recalibration pipeline.
- The verifier-constrained NAS/distillation training pipeline.
- The constrained-update/projection runtime and real-verifier checkpoint/rollback mechanism.
- The in-kernel pt-LTL/MTL monitor compiler (subformula DAG → tail-call chain + map layout).
- The verdict-aware LSM provenance/taint capture plane and graduated enforcement plane.
- The self-protection meta-monitor and heartbeat mechanism.
- The kernel-version-portable bundle format and CI build/test matrix.
- The CEGIS-style sandboxed red-team harness with a bounded adversary capability model.
- A "detection certificate" artifact format (Section 8, paper B candidate) — defined precisely enough to state exactly what it certifies (verifier loadability of the fragment loaded, versus soundness against a stated evasion class — these are different claims and the certificate must say which).

## 8. Things To Test (evaluation plan)

- **Surrogate fidelity:** agreement rate between $\hat{V}_\theta$ and the real verifier, including on adversarially mined near-boundary programs, tracked across the training run and across kernel versions.
- **Model quality under the constraint:** detection F1 of the verifier-constrained-search model versus (a) the userspace neurosymbolic core (upper bound) and (b) a hand-shrunk-then-filtered baseline (the "fit-first" approach this proposal replaces) — this comparison is the paper's central empirical result.
- **Adaptation safety:** over a sequence of simulated drift/online-update events, measure how often the projected update would have failed real-verifier loading if not projected, and confirm zero unrecoverable load failures with projection enabled.
- **Overhead:** nginx/redis under load — event loss rate, per-syscall latency (report p50/p99/p999, not mean only), CPU overhead attributable to each plane separately.
- **Detection and evasion:** F1 against labeled APT scenario datasets (e.g., DARPA Engagement-style provenance traces), and evasion rate across CEGIS red-team rounds under a stated, bounded adversary capability.
- **False positives under production load:** explicitly tracked and reported alongside overhead — a fast but noisy detector is not a deployability win.
- **Comparison systems:** Tetragon, Falco, Tracee — framed as overhead/deployability parity checks, not head-to-head detection F1, since none of them implement in-kernel temporal-logic monitoring or taint-scoped provenance.
- **Tamper resistance:** evaluate the self-protection plane against published eBPF rootkit/tampering techniques (e.g., event-pipeline interception approaches such as SPiCa-style attacks), with an explicit statement of the assumed privilege asymmetry.
- **Portability:** verifier acceptance and detection parity across several LTS kernels.

## 9. Risks and Constraints

- BPF LSM requires specific kernel config and boot parameters (`lsm=...,bpf`), limiting deployability on unmodified systems.
- The verifier's stack size, tail-call depth, and complexity limits fundamentally cap in-kernel model size; the surrogate approach optimizes *within* this cap, it does not remove it.
- The surrogate's fidelity is the load-bearing empirical result of the whole proposal — if it cannot be made reasonably faithful and well-calibrated, the central mechanism (constrained search + constrained online adaptation) does not hold, and the paper degrades to a conventional fit-first system with a differentiable pre-filter, which is a much smaller contribution.
- Self-protection guarantees are inherently relative to a privilege ordering; the paper must state that ordering precisely rather than imply unconditional tamper resistance.
- Only bounded past-time logic fragments are monitorable in-kernel; unbounded/future-time properties remain out of scope.
- This is still a lot for one paper. Recommended split: **Paper A** = Sections 2.1–2.3 (verifier-constrained search + constrained continual adaptation) plus the supporting infrastructure (Section 3), evaluated on the feasibility checks in Section 6 and the empirical tests in Section 8 excluding the certificate work. **Paper B** = Section 2.4's tighter neurosymbolic coupling plus the CEGIS/red-team loop and the detection-certificate artifact, which is a large enough formal contribution (what exactly is being certified) to stand alone.
